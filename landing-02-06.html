Abstract

Legal responses to failures of generative artificial intelligence have focused primarily on the professionals who use AI-generated material. Lawyers have been sanctioned for fabricated authorities, firms have faced malpractice exposure, and courts have emphasized duties of competence, candour, and verification. Far less attention has been paid to the independent liability of the companies that design, market, integrate, and sell professional legal AI.
